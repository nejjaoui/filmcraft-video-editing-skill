# Footage-led editing playbook

Choose a treatment from what the footage contains and what the user wants viewers to understand or feel. State the chosen style and one sentence of rationale when handing off. If the request is specific, make the edit; do not turn a clear request into a style questionnaire. For a broad brief, ask only for a missing audience or destination if the answer would materially change the edit.

## Read the footage

Before choosing a style, check:

- **Purpose and subject:** tutorial, interview, performance, event, product, story, or visual montage.
- **Coverage:** number and variety of shots, close-ups vs. wide shots, B-roll, repeated takes, and whether important action is covered from multiple angles.
- **Motion and visual rhythm:** camera movement, subject movement, screen/UI changes, and usable moments for clean cut points.
- **Sound:** speech, music, ambience, room tone, clipping, gaps, and whether quiet is intentional.
- **Delivery:** aspect ratio, platform, target length, captions or titles explicitly requested, and any source constraints.

Look at samples across the source, not just the opening. Use source audio and a transcript where useful; a waveform or decibel threshold alone cannot identify a safe speech cut. Preserve the source frame and screen state across cuts unless reframing is requested.

## Style choices by footage

| Material | Useful default | Edit choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tutorial, screen recording, or software demo | Clear, concise, step-led | Remove requested dead air and repeated attempts; preserve the order needed to understand the task, cursor movement that explains it, and UI state changes. Use clean cuts and restrained section transitions. Avoid punch-ins that obscure controls. |
| Talking head, interview, or podcast | Natural, coherent conversation | Cut only between complete thoughts; preserve emphasis, reactions, breaths, and useful pauses. Use supplied angle changes or B-roll only when they improve comprehension. J/L cuts can smooth a real scene change; don't invent coverage. |
| Product or service footage | Feature-led, benefit-focused | Lead with the strongest visual proof, then show distinct useful features in a coherent order. Favor crisp cuts or match-on-action; use titles only when requested or supplied. |
| Travel, nature, or cinematic B-roll | Mood-led, composed rhythm | Use shot scale and movement to build rhythm; match motion or color across adjacent images. Keep longer shots when atmosphere matters. Dissolve for a deliberate passage of time or mood; use direct cuts for visual energy. |
| Event, live performance, or speech | Moment-led, continuous coverage | Keep reactions, audience response, and performance beats that carry meaning. Cut on movement or phrase boundaries. Retain useful room sound and avoid fades that hide a defining moment. |
| Sports or fast action | Energetic, easy-to-follow continuity | Cut on action and preserve spatial continuity. Slow motion or speed changes need a clear source moment and user intent. Keep transitions brief and sparing. |
| Mixed or unclear footage | Simple story-first edit | Choose the clearest beginning, progression, and ending from available material. Make minimal, reversible styling choices; ask only if a real editorial decision cannot be inferred. |

These are starting points, not mandatory formulas. Do not add music, narration, captions, logos, claims, or footage that the user did not provide or request. Do not assume that “cinematic” requires letterboxing or a color grade.

## Silence and speech edits

When the user asks to remove silence, remove genuine dead air while preserving the meaning and natural cadence:

1. Inspect audio and, when available, use a transcript to find pause candidates. Discover whether the installed FilmCraft version has transcript generation or pause-removal commands before choosing them.
2. Treat thresholds and transcript gaps as candidates only. Distinguish unintentional pauses from deliberate emphasis, listening pauses in a tutorial, room tone, suspense, and quiet scene atmosphere.
3. Keep a small handle at the start and end of spoken phrases; cut at breaths or phrase boundaries rather than chopping consonants. Preserve audio/video sync by cutting linked media ranges together.
4. Use a short audio crossfade or room-tone bed only when it smooths an audible discontinuity. Keep dialogue intelligible and do not silently shorten substantial content.
5. Preview and listen around edits. Revisit any cut that clips speech, removes a useful beat, creates a click, or makes the next image/audio feel unrelated.

If the user just says “make it tighter,” remove only obvious dead air, repetitions, or mistakes that are plainly redundant. Preserve brief natural pauses unless a faster pace is clearly required.

## Transitions and pacing

- A straight cut is the default between continuous actions, dialogue lines, and screen/UI steps.
- Use a cross dissolve when the footage supports a clear change in time, place, or emotional tone, or the user explicitly requests fades. Keep it short enough that subjects and text remain readable.
- Use fade to/from black at a true beginning or ending; reserve a dip to black for a deliberate section break. Do not apply the same transition to every cut just because the user asked for “cinematic.”
- Keep transitions coherent across video and audio. Do not fade music over an edit when the original sound should continue naturally; inspect available transition commands and audio transition effects first.
- Let shot duration follow the information and movement. A complex tutorial step needs time to read; an action beat can cut quickly; a landscape may need a longer hold.

## Final review

Review the opening, ending, every new scene type, representative middle sections, and a sample of edits. Check that the selected style does not obscure text/UI, flatten important performance, change the tone unexpectedly, or create audio jumps. Verify exact duration and output aspect ratio against the user's request. Report any unsupported treatment or edit that the available footage could not support.
